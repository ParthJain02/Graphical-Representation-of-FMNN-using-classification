# Graph-Structure-Aware Hyperbox Refinement

## Abstract

This project evaluates a two-stage node-classification pipeline: a graph convolutional network (GCN) produces node embeddings, and a fuzzy min-max neural network (FMNN) classifies those embeddings with hyperboxes. The proposed graph-structure-aware hyperbox refinement (GSHR) applies graph and geometric constraints to merge or prune FMNN hyperboxes.

The implementation is reproducible on the public Cora and CiteSeer Planetoid splits. Across the completed Cora ablation, refinement reduced hyperbox count, but full GSHR did not improve predictive performance over the FMNN baseline. The result supports a compactness trade-off hypothesis, not a claim of accuracy improvement or research novelty.

## Architecture

1. Load a transductive Planetoid graph and validate disjoint masks.
2. Train a two-layer GCN using training labels only.
3. Select the checkpoint by validation Macro-F1.
4. Extract 64-dimensional node embeddings.
5. Fit min-max normalization on training embeddings only and clip held-out embeddings.
6. Fit Hyperbox-Brain FMNN on training embeddings.
7. Optionally merge or prune hyperboxes using validation-only decisions.
8. Evaluate once on the held-out test mask.

Hyperboxes describe regions in learned embedding space. They do not directly explain original input features.

## Experimental Protocol

Cora uses the public Planetoid split: 2,708 nodes, 1,433 features, 7 classes, and the standard 140/500/1,000 train/validation/test masks. CiteSeer uses the corresponding public Planetoid split. Test labels are not used for checkpoint selection, hyperparameter selection, merging, pruning, or variant selection.

The Cora E1-E8 matrix uses five seeds and is stored in `results/tables/ablation_summary_corrected.json`. Figures are generated by `scripts/evaluate.py` from that artifact.

## Cora Results

| Variant | Mean accuracy | Mean Macro-F1 | Mean boxes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| E1 GCN + softmax | 0.8042 | 0.7946 | 0.0 |
| E2 GCN + FMNN | 0.7528 | 0.7391 | 87.4 |
| E3 geometry-only merge | 0.7436 | 0.7367 | 45.4 |
| E4 graph-aware merge | 0.7542 | 0.7395 | 45.4 |
| E5 graph-aware pruning | 0.7528 | 0.7391 | 87.4 |
| E6 full GSHR | 0.7492 | 0.7288 | 43.8 |
| E7 graph-disabled full refinement | 0.7630 | 0.7446 | 43.2 |
| E8 unsafe diagnostic refinement | 0.7492 | 0.7288 | 43.8 |

Relative to the FMNN baseline, E6 reduces the mean box count by approximately 49.9% but reduces mean Macro-F1 by approximately 0.0103. E7 performs better than E6 in this run, so the results do not isolate a positive contribution from graph association.

## CiteSeer Results

The frozen Cora-selected configuration was evaluated without retuning. Results are in `results/tables/final_evaluation_citeseer_current.json`.

- FMNN baseline: accuracy 0.6288, Macro-F1 0.6130, 9.8 boxes.
- Full GSHR: accuracy 0.6282, Macro-F1 0.6142, 7.8 boxes.
- Pruning-only: accuracy 0.6350, Macro-F1 0.6069, 9.6 boxes.

The CiteSeer result shows a small Macro-F1 increase and a small accuracy decrease for full GSHR. This is inconsistent evidence, not a general performance claim.

## Limitations

- The GSHR scoring and pruning rules are research implementations, not established validated methods.
- The current refinement implementation does not yet provide a fully independent parameter sweep for every graph-association weight.
- The validation sets are small relative to the graphs, so practical selection uncertainty is substantial.
- Results cover two citation benchmarks and five seeds; broader heterophilous and robustness evaluations remain future work.
- E8 is intentionally unsafe and must not be interpreted as a deployment configuration.

## Conclusion

The completed evidence supports the narrower conclusion that hyperbox refinement can reduce FMNN representation size on these runs. It does not support claiming that graph-aware refinement improves node-classification quality. The next scientifically useful work is better calibrated refinement objectives, class- and seed-level error analysis, and additional heterophilous benchmarks.
